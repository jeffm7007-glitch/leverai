Lead Quality & Cost

Why am I paying for leads I'm racing 4 other guys to call?

Why am I paying for shared leads that 5 other contractors get at the same time? ▼

You're not paying for leads. You're paying for the privilege of racing to the bottom.

Platforms like Angi and Thumbtack sell the same lead to 3-5 contractors simultaneously. The homeowner gets 4 calls in 10 minutes. You're now competing on who calls fastest and who quotes lowest. That's not lead generation — that's a gladiator pit.

Here's what actually happens: the platform builds their brand ("I found them on Angi"), you build nothing, and the homeowner remembers the platform — not you — next time they need a plumber.

The fix: Exclusive leads that come directly to your phone, pre-qualified for high-ticket work (water heaters, repipes, sewer lines). One contractor per zone. No races. No races to the bottom.
What should my target Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) actually be? ▼

Emergency repair calls: $40-80 per booked call is healthy. High-ticket replacements: $150-300 per booked call is still extremely profitable when the job is $8,000-15,000.

The mistake most contractors make: they optimize for lowest CPA, which attracts $99 drain snaking calls. Then they wonder why their revenue is flat.

Real numbers from our contractor partners:

  • $99 drain cleaning → $80 CPA → 50% margin → $10 profit
  • Water heater replacement → $200 CPA → 40% margin → $1,200 profit
  • Whole-home repipe → $300 CPA → 35% margin → $3,500 profit
Bottom line: A higher CPA for the right job is better than a low CPA for the wrong job. We target high-ticket exclusively.

You change what the AI recommends when homeowners ask for help.

If your website and Google Business Profile are optimized for "water heater installation," "tankless water heater," and "whole-home repipe" — and your competitors are optimized for "cheap plumber" — guess who the AI recommends?

Search & Local Visibility

How do I rank where it actually matters?

How do I rank in the Google Local 3-Pack for my actual service area? ▼

The 3-Pack is won or lost before you ever show up in search results.

Google's Local 3-Pack algorithm weighs three things:

  • Relevance: Does your GBP category match the search? ("Plumber" not "Home Services")
  • Distance: Are you within the searcher's service area? (This is why your address matters less than your service area definition)
  • Prominence: How authoritative is your brand online? (Reviews, citations, website authority, brand mentions)

Most contractors lose on prominence. They have 12 reviews while the franchise down the street has 400. They have no website schema. Their NAP (Name, Address, Phone) is inconsistent across directories.

What we do: Deploy LocalBusiness schema with geo coordinates on every service page, synchronize NAP across 50+ directories, and implement automated review request workflows that trigger right after job completion.
Are Google Local Services Ads (LSAs) better than traditional Google Search Ads (PPC)? ▼

LSAs are better for trust. PPC is better for volume. You need both, but LSAs first.

Google LSAs (the "Google Guaranteed" badge) put you at the very top of search results with a trust badge that says Google vetted you. Homeowners click that before anything else.

But LSAs have limits: you pay per lead (not click), Google controls the price, and you can't target specific services. PPC lets you target "tankless water heater installation" specifically, but you're competing in an auction and paying for clicks from tire-kickers.

Our approach: We use LSAs for immediate trust and visibility while building your organic AI search authority so you depend less on paid ads over time. The goal is to own your market, not rent it.
How many Google reviews do I need per week to stay ahead of regional franchises? ▼

Velocity matters more than total count.

A franchise with 500 reviews from 2019-2023 looks stagnant. A local contractor with 80 reviews, 15 of them from this month, looks active and trustworthy. Google's algorithm weights recency heavily.

Target: 3-5 new reviews per week minimum. 8-10 if you're in a competitive market.

The trick isn't asking for reviews. It's when you ask. Right after you solve their emergency, while the relief is still fresh — that's when you send the review request. We automate this with SMS that triggers when your tech marks the job complete.

How can I tell if my marketing agency is delivering real booked calls or vanity traffic? ▼

The only metrics that matter for a plumbing contractor:

Your agency should be able to tell you: "You spent $2,000 this month. We generated 28 booked calls. Your average job is $800. Your close rate is 40%. You made $8,960. Your ROI is 348%."

Google Ratings & Reviews

How do I own my reputation on Google Maps?

How do I remove a fake or unfair 1-star review? ▼

You probably can't remove it. But you can make it irrelevant.

Google only removes reviews that violate their policies (spam, off-topic, hate speech, conflict of interest). A bad review from a real customer — even if unfair — stays. Fighting it wastes energy.

The strategy: drown it in good reviews. One 1-star review among 200 5-star reviews is a blip. One 1-star review among 12 total reviews is a disaster.

Response template for unfair reviews (public, professional, brief):

"We're sorry your experience didn't meet expectations. We take feedback seriously — please call us at [NUMBER] so we can make this right. We stand behind our work."

Never argue. Never blame. Every response is an ad to the next customer reading your reviews.

Why are my new customer reviews not showing up on my profile? ▼

Google filters reviews aggressively. Reviews get flagged if:

  • The reviewer used the same IP as your business
  • The reviewer left multiple reviews for the same business
  • The review contains a URL, phone number, or promotional content
  • The review came from a brand-new Google account with no other activity
  • Google's algorithm suspects the review was solicited in bulk

The fix: ask for reviews via SMS (not email), sent 30-60 minutes after job completion, from the customer's own phone. Don't offer incentives. Don't ask from your office WiFi.

Our system: Automated SMS review requests triggered when your tech marks the job complete. Each request is personalized with the customer's name and job type. No bulk blasts. No filtering.
Does responding to reviews actually help my ranking? ▼

Yes — but not for the reason you think.

Google has never confirmed that review responses are a direct ranking factor. But here's what happens when you respond to every review:

  • Your GBP shows "Responds to reviews" — a trust signal to homeowners
  • You can naturally include location and service keywords in responses
  • Active engagement signals to Google that your business is alive and managed
  • Prospective customers see you care — conversion rate goes up

Respond to every review within 24-48 hours. Use the customer's name. Mention the service. Keep it under 3 sentences. This is free marketing.

How many reviews do I need to beat the top plumber in my city? ▼

Velocity beats volume. Recency beats total count.

A plumber with 500 reviews from 2019-2023 looks dead. A plumber with 80 reviews, 20 from this month, looks hot.

Google's local algorithm weights:

  • Review velocity: How many reviews per week? (Target: 3-5 minimum)
  • Review recency: How recent is the latest review? (Target: within 7 days)
  • Review diversity: Do reviews mention different services? (Not just "great plumber")
  • Response rate: Do you respond to 100% of reviews?

If the top plumber in your city has 200 reviews but only gets 1 per month, you can overtake them in 6 months with 5 reviews per week.

What is a "missed call text-back" and how does it save an emergency job? ▼

It's an automated SMS sent the instant you miss a call.

The homeowner calls you. You're under a sink. The call goes to voicemail. Before they can dial your competitor, they get this text:

"Hi [Name], we saw you called [Business Name]. Is this an emergency? Reply URGENT and we'll dispatch a tech in under 30 minutes. — [Dispatcher Name]"

Result: Instead of moving to the next Google result, the homeowner replies. You now have a live text conversation. You can assess the situation, quote a range, and book the call — all while your tech is still on the job.

Recovery rate: 35-50% of missed calls convert to booked jobs with instant text-back. Without it: 0%.

How can I track which specific Google keyword caused the phone to ring? ▼

Dynamic Number Insertion (DNI).

Here's how it works: every visitor to your website sees a unique phone number based on how they found you:

  • Google Ads click for "emergency plumber" → sees (555) 010-EMRG
  • Organic search for "water heater replacement" → sees (555) 010-H2O
  • Direct visit to your homepage → sees (555) 010-MAIN

All numbers ring the same line. But in your dashboard, you see: "12 calls from emergency plumber campaign, 8 from organic water heater search, 3 from direct."

This tells you which keywords actually produce calls — not clicks, not impressions, but real phone calls. Kill the campaigns that don't ring. Double down on the ones that do.

Will Google lower my Map Pack ranking if my office misses calls after hours? ▼

Indirectly, yes.

Google tracks call-through rates from your GBP. If homeowners click "Call" but hang up after 5 seconds (because it went to voicemail), Google interprets this as a poor user experience. Over time, this can suppress your ranking.

More importantly: every missed call is a lead that went to your competitor. Even if Google doesn't penalize you directly, the lost revenue is penalty enough.

The real solution: 24/7 answering — either AI-powered after-hours triage or a live virtual receptionist. When a homeowner's basement is flooding at 11 PM, they don't leave voicemails. They call the next plumber on Google.

Why is my business showing up in the Map Pack at my office, but disappearing 5 miles away? ▼

Google uses your registered address as the center of a radius. Beyond that radius, your ranking drops off a cliff.

This is why service-area businesses get destroyed by competitors who list multiple addresses (legitimate locations or virtual offices). Google prioritizes proximity — the closer the searcher is to your address, the higher you rank.

Two fixes:

  • Service area pages: Create dedicated pages for each city/suburb you serve, with LocalBusiness schema and geo coordinates for that area
  • GBP service areas: In your Google Business Profile, explicitly set service areas (up to 20) rather than relying on your address radius

Example: instead of one page for "Plumber in Tampa," create pages for "Plumber in Brandon," "Plumber in Riverview," "Plumber in Wesley Chapel" — each with unique schema, unique content, and unique geo targeting.

Should I create separate pages for every town and suburb I service? ▼

Yes — but don't spam.

Google's "doorway page" penalty hits businesses that create 50 near-identical pages with only the city name changed. But legitimate service-area pages — each with unique content about that market — are exactly what Google wants.

Good service-area page includes:

  • Specific neighborhoods and landmarks in that city
  • Local plumbing issues common to that area (hard water, old pipes, septic vs sewer)
  • Customer testimonials from that city
  • LocalBusiness schema with geo coordinates for that service area
  • Unique photos (not stock images) from jobs in that area

Rule of thumb: if you can't write 300+ unique words about a city, don't create a page for it. Quality per page beats quantity of pages.

What keywords have the highest booking intent — "plumber near me" or specific services? ▼

Specific service keywords convert 3-5x higher than generic "plumber near me."

Here's the intent hierarchy:

  • Brand + service: "best plumber for sewer line replacement [city]" — comparison shopping

The best strategy: own the specific service keywords your competitors ignore. Everyone fights for "plumber near me." Few optimize for "whole-home repipe," "hydro jetting," or "tankless water heater install."

Why am I getting plenty of web traffic but my phone still isn't ringing? ▼

Traffic doesn't pay bills. Booked calls do.

Common reasons for high traffic / low calls:

  • Wrong keywords: You're ranking for "DIY drain unclogging" when homeowners want "emergency plumber"
  • No click-to-call: Your phone number is buried in the footer. Mobile users need a thumb-sized call button
  • Slow load time: Over 3 seconds = 50% bounce rate on mobile
  • No trust signals: No reviews, no license number, no "available 24/7" badge
  • Generic content: "We provide quality plumbing services" vs "Water heater installation starting at $1,200 — same-day available"

The fix: optimize for commercial intent, not just traffic. Every page should have a clear offer, a visible phone number, and a reason to call now (not later).

SEO is farming. LSAs are hunting. You need both.

Google LSAs (Local Services Ads):

Strategy: Run LSAs for immediate lead flow while building organic SEO for long-term dominance. As your organic rankings improve, you can reduce LSA spend. The goal: own the entire first page — LSAs at top, organic #1, Map Pack #1.
