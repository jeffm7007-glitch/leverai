I used to think AI was expensive. Then I built something that proved me wrong.

Four months ago I started ProPlum Network to help plumbers get found online. I needed to manage 200 city pages, respond to reviews, research competitors, and qualify leads. A marketing agency quoted me $3,000 a month. I don't have $3,000 a month. I'm bootstrapping this.

So I found another way.

What I Actually Built

It's not fancy. It's 500 lines of JavaScript that does one thing: takes a task, picks the right free AI model for that task, and returns the result.

That's it. No custom agents. No training. No API bills.

Here's what happened when I tested it on four real tasks:

The Part Nobody Talks About

Here's what every US agency is missing: the Chinese models are better for specific jobs, and they're free.

DeepSeek scored 89.2% on standard benchmarks. GPT-4 scored 86.4%. DeepSeek is open source, so I can run it locally when a client doesn't want their data leaving my server. Try doing that with OpenAI.

Kimi has a context window five times longer than Claude's. I can feed it an entire city's competitive landscape — fifty reviews, twenty pages, six months of data — and get analysis back in one shot.

Qwen is five to seventeen times cheaper than GPT-4 and handles Spanish natively. Hispanic contractors are massively underserved. I can reach them without paying translation costs.

And here's the kicker: most US agencies don't even know these exist. They're still debating Claude versus GPT-4 while I'm routing tasks to six models they haven't heard of.

My clients never see model names. They see results. If DeepSeek writes a better Google review response than Gemini, I use it. End of story.

Why Cloudflare Matters

I run this on Cloudflare Workers, which isn't just hosting. It's 300+ cities worldwide. My orchestrator doesn't live in one data center. It lives everywhere my plumbers are.

And the crazy part? It's free. Workers, Durable Objects, KV storage, queues, analytics — all free tier. Zero dollars for infrastructure.

What I'm Actually Building
